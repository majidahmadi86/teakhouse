# Teak House · visual bible

The one written direction for every photograph on the Teak House site (directive v17, A2 and A4).
It is binding for every slot in `docs/image-briefs.json`. If a photograph would not survive this
page, it does not go on the site, however good it is on its own.

## 1. The place

One bright, contemporary five-star hotel in a riverside city like Bangkok, standing on the bank of a
wide, slow, grey-green river. The hotel is fictional: it is never a real building and never shown as
one. The city is present as light, a soft skyline across the water and green on the far bank, never
as a named place.

Mood references (mood only, never their photographs or buildings): Park Hyatt, Kimpton Maa-Lai,
Anantara, Mandarin Oriental. Calm, generous, airy, welcoming. Rooms and public spaces look ready for
you: made, lit by the day, uncluttered.

Teak House is not Solenne. Solenne is an ultra-luxury estate of pale limestone pavilions, bleached
teak and reflecting pools, graded toward bone and champagne. Teak House is a city hotel: warmer wood,
crisper white, more glass, more green, more river and city light. It is also not the rejected v15
(espresso, gold, brass gradients, dark candle-lit rooms).

## 2. Architecture and materials

- **Warm teak**: louvres, screens, panelling, floors, doors, furniture. Honey to mid-brown, oiled,
  with visible grain. Never bleached grey, never dark lacquer.
- **White stone**: white or ivory marble with soft veining, white terrazzo, white render. Crisp,
  clean, cool against the teak.
- **Glass**: floor-to-ceiling windows and sliding doors that let the river and the city in.
- **Tropical green**: palms, monstera, ferns, banana leaf, a mature mango tree, white and green
  orchids, jasmine, lotus in a bowl. Planting is lush but tidy.
- **Accents**: brass in small doses (lamps, handles, a service bell), cane and rattan, celadon
  ceramics, white linen, oatmeal and sand textiles, a little river-green.
- **Form**: contemporary tropical modernism. A mid-rise building with deep balconies and vertical
  teak louvres, a river terrace, a pier, a courtyard pool, a rooftop pool. Low pavilions only where a
  brief asks for one (the event pavilion), and never as a limestone estate.

## 3. Light

- **Default: bright daylight**, morning to mid-afternoon. High-key and airy. Windows read bright;
  only sky may clip to white. Shadows stay open and soft.
- **Golden hour and dusk only where the brief says evening**: Loy Krathong, the long-tail ride, the
  sundowner party strip, the rail cocktails, the early-bird sunrise. Even then: clean, warm, readable,
  never murky.
- **Never**: haze, smog, fog, smoke, heavy vignette, crushed blacks, dramatic flare, HDR halos,
  teal-and-orange grading, night interiors lit by candles, dim moody hotel rooms.

## 4. Colour

Natural colour with true whites and a gentle warmth (a neutral to slightly warm daylight look). No
heavy grade, no desaturated monochrome, no electric skies.

| Role | Words | Approx. hex |

| Linen | warm off-white | #EAE3D6 |
| Sand | oatmeal textiles, rattan | #D8C7AC |
| Light teak | honey | #C89A6A |
| Teak | mid-brown, oiled | #A0673A |
| Tropical green | palm and leaf | #3F6B4A |
| Fresh leaf | lit foliage | #6E9A63 |

grade, and v15's gold on espresso (#C8A24E on #23201B).

## 5. Composition and crops

Every master is at least 3000 px wide (portrait slots: 3000 px on the long side), never upscaled,
sharp at 100 percent, horizon level and verticals straight.

**Home hero** (16:9 desktop plus a 4:5 phone crop of the same photograph)
- The focal subject (pool edge, terrace, facade) sits inside the central 50 percent of the width and
  the middle-to-lower third of the height, so the 4:5 crop (centre-weighted, may shift up to 15
  percent sideways) still holds it whole.
- Keep a calm area top-left (sky, wall or water) for the headline. Overlays only as strong as
  legibility needs.
- The phone crop of a 4000 x 2667 master is about 2130 x 2667, enough for a 412 x 823 box at 3x.

**Page heroes** (16:9 desktop; phone crop about 1:1 to 6:7, around 420 x 440 to 490 CSS px)
- Subject centred, with nothing important in the outer 20 percent on either side.
- One idea per frame. A calm band for the title.

**Rooms** (cover 4:3 card, 16:10 room page, 16:9 /book mobile, all from one master; bath, view and
detail at 4:3)
- Eye level about 1.1 to 1.3 m, one- or two-point perspective, verticals corrected.
- The bed or the room's signature feature in the left-to-centre two thirds; the window and its light
  visible in the cover.
- The four frames of one room come from one shoot wherever possible (same finishes, same light).

**Venues and experiences** (4:3 or 3:2; dining category strips 16:7)
- 16:7 strips: the subject must sit in the central horizontal band (about 44 percent of the height)
  so the strip crop keeps it.
- Wide enough to read the space, close enough to feel it. Empty and ready, never a crowd.

**Dishes** (1:1, served at 64 CSS px from 128 and 256 px files)
- Overhead or 45 degrees, one plate or glass centred with about 15 percent margin, on teak or white
  stone, in daylight. A clear silhouette that reads at thumbnail size; no clutter around it.

**Details** (4:3 or 1:1): a single subject, shallow depth allowed, texture and light first.

**Account panel**: portrait (45vw by full height), a vertical subject.

**OG share image**: a 1200 x 630 (1.91:1) crop with a centred subject and safe margins; nothing
baked in.

**Gallery**: 4:3, 3:2 and 4:5 mixed; every frame distinct and from the new set.

## 6. People

No face in focus, ever. Hands are allowed where a brief asks for them (a cook cutting mango, a
server setting down a bowl, a therapist's hands). Far, soft, unidentifiable figures only if a brief
allows them. No crowds, no posed groups, no guests in swimwear.

## 7. What never appears

- **Text of any kind**: signs, menus, labels, book spines, legible screens, room or gate numbers,
  price tags, watermarks.
- **Logos and brand marks**: on bottles, robes, amenities, cars, luggage, umbrellas. No licence
  plates.
- **Real or identifiable properties**: any photo whose description, location or tags name a hotel,
  resort or villa; any visible branding of one; any signature building or interior that a reader
  could name.
- **Landmarks**: temples and their prangs (Wat Arun and the like), the Grand Palace, named towers
  (for example MahaNakhon, ICONSIAM, the State Tower dome), Marina Bay Sands, famous bridges, royal
  portraits or billboards.
- **The wrong place**: beaches, sea horizons, karst islands, mountains, deserts, snow, European old
  towns. This is a river city.
- **Tourist boats and crowds**: express boats, ferries with passengers, dinner-cruise boats, rows of
  long-tails, crowded promenades.
- **Budget or dated cues**: a television as the hero of a room, patterned carpet, kitchenettes,
  plastic furniture, street-stall tables, heavy chandeliers, dark wood with gold.
- **Solenne or v15 looks**: limestone pavilions on reflecting pools, bleached monochrome grade,
  frangipani as a motif; espresso and gold, brass gradients, candle-lit dark rooms.
- **Fakes**: CGI renders, AI artefacts, warped lines, odd hands, composites.

## 8. Brightness thresholds

True greyscale mean, measured as `sharp(file).resize(64,64,{fit:'inside'}).greyscale().raw()` and
the mean of the bytes (not `.stats()`, which reports the red channel).

| Slot type | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Interiors, details, dishes | 120 |
| Exteriors and every hero (including the OG image) | 135 |
| Slots whose brief says evening or dawn | 100, and never murky |

Bands for reporting: bright 140 and up, mid 100 to 139, dark under 100. Record each chosen frame's
dominant tone hex (the 64 px mean, lifted toward the frame's ambient light): it becomes the colour
ground behind the image before it decodes.

## 9. Licence and records

- Unsplash License only, with `premium === false` and `plus === false`; never Unsplash+ or Getty. The
  Pexels License is also acceptable.
- Every chosen file goes into `docs/image-ledger.md` with slot, photo page, author, licence, original
  URL, master size, brightness and tone.

## 10. Scoring and pass rule

Each candidate is viewed and scored 1 to 5 on relevance to its brief, brightness and high-key light,
realism, consistency with this bible, and technical quality. A slot passes only with relevance 4 or
more, brightness 4 or more, realism 5, bible 4 or more, quality 4 or more, and the brightness
threshold above. At least three genuine candidates are judged per slot. If nothing passes after a
fair search, the slot is marked unfilled with what was tried; a weak or placeholder image never
fills a slot.
